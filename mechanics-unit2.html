1. Force

Force is an external agent which changes or tends to change the state of rest or motion of a body.

Force = Mass × Acceleration
F = ma

SI Unit: Newton (N)

2. Characteristics of Force

A force is completely described by:

  • Magnitude
  • Direction
  • Line of action
  • Point of application

3. Types of Force

  • Contact Force
  • Non-contact Force
  • Concentrated Force
  • Distributed Force
  • Coplanar Force
  • Non-coplanar Force

4. Coplanar Force System

When all forces acting on a body lie in the same plane, the system is called a coplanar force system.

Types

  • Collinear forces
  • Concurrent forces
  • Parallel forces
  • General coplanar forces

5. Collinear Forces

Forces whose lines of action lie along the same straight line are called collinear forces.

Example: Forces acting in the same straight line on a rod.

6. Concurrent Forces

Forces whose lines of action meet at one common point are called concurrent forces.

7. Parallel Forces

Forces whose lines of action are parallel to each other are called parallel forces.

Types

  • Like parallel forces
  • Unlike parallel forces

8. Resolution of Force

The process of replacing a single force by two or more component forces is called resolution of force.

Fx = F cos θ

Fy = F sin θ

9. Resultant Force

The single force which produces the same effect as a system of forces is called the resultant force.

R = √(Fx² + Fy²)
tan θ = Fy / Fx

10. Principle of Superposition

The combined effect of several forces is equal to the vector sum of their individual effects.

11. Lami's Theorem

If a body is in equilibrium under three concurrent forces, then each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two forces.

P/sin α = Q/sin β = R/sin γ
Important: Lami's theorem is applicable to three concurrent forces in equilibrium.

12. Varignon's Theorem

The moment of the resultant force about any point is equal to the algebraic sum of the moments of its component forces about the same point.

Moment = Force × Perpendicular Distance

13. Moment of a Force

The turning effect of a force about a point is called the moment of the force.

M = F × d

Where F = force and d = perpendicular distance from the point.

SI Unit: N·m

14. Equilibrium of Forces

A body is said to be in equilibrium when the resultant force and resultant moment acting on it are zero.

ΣFx = 0
ΣFy = 0
ΣM = 0

15. Solved Numerical

Example 1

A force of 100 N acts at an angle of 30° with the horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical components.

Fx = F cos θ
Fx = 100 cos 30°
Fx = 86.6 N
Fy = F sin θ
Fy = 100 sin 30°
Fy = 50 N
Answer: Horizontal component = 86.6 N
Vertical component = 50 N

18. Quick Revision

Force: F = ma

Horizontal component: Fx = F cos θ

Vertical component: Fy = F sin θ

Moment: M = F × d

Equilibrium: ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0, ΣM = 0

Lami: P/sin α = Q/sin β = R/sin γ
