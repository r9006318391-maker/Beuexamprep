1. Equilibrium

A body is said to be in equilibrium when the resultant force and resultant moment acting on the body are zero.

For equilibrium:

ΣF = 0
ΣM = 0

2. Types of Equilibrium

1. Static Equilibrium

A body is at rest and all forces and moments are balanced.

2. Dynamic Equilibrium

A body moves with constant velocity while the resultant force acting on it is zero.

Exam Point:
Static equilibrium means the body is at rest.

3. Conditions of Equilibrium

For a body in 2D equilibrium:

ΣFx = 0

ΣFy = 0

ΣM = 0

These three equations are used to find unknown forces and support reactions.

4. Free Body Diagram (FBD)

A Free Body Diagram is a simplified diagram showing all external forces and moments acting on a body.

Steps to Draw FBD

  1. Isolate the body from its surroundings.
  2. Show all applied forces.
  3. Show support reactions.
  4. Show weight of the body.
  5. Show dimensions if required.
  6. Select suitable x and y directions.
Important: Correct FBD is the first step for solving equilibrium problems.

5. Types of Supports

1. Roller Support

A roller support generally provides one reaction perpendicular to the supporting surface.

2. Pin/Hinged Support

A pin support can provide horizontal and vertical reactions.

3. Fixed Support

A fixed support can provide horizontal reaction, vertical reaction and a moment reaction.

6. Simply Supported Beam

A simply supported beam is generally supported by a pin at one end and a roller at the other end.

Support Reactions

Let the reactions at the supports be:

Vertical reaction at A = Ay
Vertical reaction at B = By

For equilibrium:

ΣFy = 0
Ay + By - Total Load = 0

7. Moment of a Force

The turning effect of a force about a point is called the moment of force.

Moment = Force × Perpendicular Distance

M = F × d

SI unit of moment is N·m.

Sign Convention

  • Anticlockwise moment → Positive
  • Clockwise moment → Negative

8. Lami's Theorem

If a particle is in equilibrium under the action of three coplanar concurrent forces, then each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two forces.

F₁ / sin α = F₂ / sin β = F₃ / sin γ

Lami's theorem is useful for solving problems involving three concurrent forces.

9. Varignon's Theorem

Varignon's theorem states that the moment of the resultant force about any point is equal to the algebraic sum of the moments of its component forces about the same point.

Moment of Resultant = Sum of Moments of Components

10. Solved Numerical – Support Reaction

Question:
A simply supported beam carries a load of 10 kN at its centre. Find the reactions at both supports.

Solution:

Due to symmetry, the load is equally shared by both supports.

Ay = By

Total load = 10 kN

Therefore,

Ay + By = 10

2Ay = 10

Ay = 5 kN

By = 5 kN

Answer: Reaction at each support = 5 kN

12. MCQs

Q1. For equilibrium, the resultant force is:
A) Maximum
B) Zero
C) Infinite
D) Negative
Answer: B) Zero
Q2. SI unit of moment is:
A) N
B) J
C) N·m
D) Pa
Answer: C) N·m
Q3. FBD stands for:
A) Force Balance Diagram
B) Free Body Diagram
C) Force Body Design
D) Free Beam Design
Answer: B) Free Body Diagram
Q4. Lami's theorem is used for:
A) Three concurrent forces in equilibrium
B) Temperature
C) Heat transfer
D) Fluid flow
Answer: A) Three concurrent forces in equilibrium
Q5. Moment is equal to:
A) Force × Distance
B) Force / Distance
C) Distance / Force
D) Force + Distance
Answer: A) Force × Distance
Q6. A fixed support provides:
A) Only horizontal reaction
B) Only vertical reaction
C) Horizontal, vertical and moment reactions
D) No reaction
Answer: C) Horizontal, vertical and moment reactions

13. Quick Revision

  • Equilibrium → Resultant force and moment are zero.
  • ΣFx = 0
  • ΣFy = 0
  • ΣM = 0
  • FBD → Free Body Diagram.
  • Moment = Force × Perpendicular Distance.
  • Unit of moment → N·m.
  • Lami's theorem → Three concurrent forces.
  • Varignon's theorem → Moment of resultant.
  • Simply supported beam → Pin + Roller support.
