1. Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is a quantity that can be measured and expressed by a numerical value and a unit.

Example: Length = 5 metre

Here, 5 is the numerical value and metre is the unit.

2. Fundamental Quantities

Fundamental quantities are independent physical quantities which cannot be expressed in terms of other quantities.

  • Length – metre (m)
  • Mass – kilogram (kg)
  • Time – second (s)
  • Electric current – ampere (A)
  • Temperature – kelvin (K)
  • Amount of substance – mole (mol)
  • Luminous intensity – candela (cd)

3. Derived Quantities

Quantities which are obtained from fundamental quantities are called derived quantities.

  • Velocity = Length / Time
  • Acceleration = Length / Time²
  • Force = Mass × Acceleration
  • Work = Force × Distance
  • Pressure = Force / Area

4. SI Units

SI stands for International System of Units. It is the internationally accepted system of measurement.

  • Length → metre (m)
  • Mass → kilogram (kg)
  • Time → second (s)
  • Force → newton (N)
  • Energy → joule (J)
  • Power → watt (W)
  • Pressure → pascal (Pa)

5. Dimensions

Dimensions represent a physical quantity in terms of fundamental quantities such as Mass (M), Length (L) and Time (T).

Example:

Velocity = LT⁻¹
Acceleration = LT⁻²
Force = MLT⁻²

6. Dimensional Formula

The expression which shows the powers of fundamental quantities involved in a physical quantity is called its dimensional formula.

Important Dimensional Formulae

Velocity = [M⁰L¹T⁻¹]
Acceleration = [M⁰L¹T⁻²]
Force = [M¹L¹T⁻²]
Work = [M¹L²T⁻²]
Power = [M¹L²T⁻³]
Pressure = [M¹L⁻¹T⁻²]
Density = [M¹L⁻³T⁰]

7. Dimensional Equation

An equation which represents a physical quantity in terms of its dimensions is called a dimensional equation.

Force = [M¹L¹T⁻²]

8. Principle of Homogeneity

According to the principle of homogeneity, the dimensions of every term on both sides of a physical equation must be the same.

Example: s = ut + ½at²

Dimension of s = L
Dimension of ut = LT⁻¹ × T = L
Dimension of at² = LT⁻² × T² = L

9. Dimensional Analysis

Dimensional analysis is a mathematical method used to study the relationship between physical quantities using their dimensions.

Uses

  • To check dimensional correctness of an equation.
  • To derive a relation between physical quantities.
  • To convert units from one system to another.

10. Applications of Dimensional Analysis

  • Checking the correctness of equations.
  • Deriving physical relations.
  • Converting units.
  • Finding dimensions of unknown quantities.

11. Limitations of Dimensional Analysis

  • It cannot determine numerical constants.
  • It cannot determine whether terms are added or subtracted.
  • It cannot be used directly for trigonometric functions.
  • It cannot distinguish quantities having the same dimensions.

12. Important Formulae

Force = Mass × Acceleration
Work = Force × Distance
Power = Work / Time
Pressure = Force / Area
Density = Mass / Volume
