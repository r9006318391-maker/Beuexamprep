1. Introduction to Friction

Friction is the resisting force which opposes the relative motion or tendency of motion between two surfaces in contact.

Friction always acts opposite to the direction of motion or impending motion.

2. Causes of Friction

  • Surface irregularities
  • Interlocking of rough surfaces
  • Adhesion between contacting surfaces
  • Deformation of surfaces

3. Types of Friction

1. Static Friction

Static friction acts when there is no relative motion between the contacting surfaces.

2. Limiting Friction

The maximum value of static friction, just before motion starts, is called limiting friction.

3. Kinetic or Dynamic Friction

Friction acting when one surface is actually moving relative to another surface is called kinetic friction.

4. Rolling Friction

The resistance offered to a body when it rolls over a surface is called rolling friction.

4. Laws of Friction

  1. Friction acts opposite to the direction of motion.
  2. Limiting friction is proportional to the normal reaction.
  3. Friction depends on the nature of the contacting surfaces.
  4. Within ordinary limits, friction is approximately independent of the apparent area of contact.
F ∝ N

5. Coefficient of Friction

The ratio of limiting friction to normal reaction is called the coefficient of friction.

μ = F / N

Where:

  • μ = Coefficient of friction
  • F = Limiting friction
  • N = Normal reaction

Coefficient of friction has no unit.

6. Angle of Friction

The angle made by the resultant reaction with the normal reaction when the body is just about to move is called the angle of friction.

tan φ = μ

Where φ is the angle of friction.

7. Angle of Repose

The minimum angle of inclination of a plane at which a body just begins to slide down the plane is called the angle of repose.

tan θ = μ
Important:
Angle of repose and angle of friction are equal:

θ = φ

8. Friction on a Horizontal Plane

Consider a body of weight W resting on a horizontal surface.

N = W

F = μN

Therefore:
F = μW

Where N is the normal reaction.

9. Friction on an Inclined Plane

When a body rests on an inclined plane, its weight can be resolved into two components.

Component along plane = W sin θ

Component perpendicular to plane = W cos θ

Normal reaction:
N = W cos θ

At limiting equilibrium:

F = μN

F = μW cos θ

10. Friction Angle and Resultant Reaction

The normal reaction and friction force combine to produce a resultant reaction.

R = √(N² + F²)

The angle between R and N is called the angle of friction.

11. Advantages of Friction

  • It allows us to walk.
  • It helps vehicles to move on roads.
  • It allows brakes to work.
  • It helps in gripping objects.
  • It helps belts and pulleys transmit motion.

12. Disadvantages of Friction

  • Causes wear and tear.
  • Produces heat.
  • Causes energy loss.
  • Reduces efficiency of machines.
  • Requires additional power.

13. Methods of Reducing Friction

  • Use of lubricants
  • Use of ball bearings
  • Use of rollers
  • Polishing of surfaces
  • Using suitable materials

14. Solved Numerical

Question:
A block weighing 100 N rests on a horizontal surface. If the coefficient of friction is 0.3, find the limiting friction.

Given:

W = 100 N
μ = 0.3

For a horizontal surface:

N = W = 100 N

Limiting friction:

F = μN

F = 0.3 × 100

F = 30 N

Answer: Limiting friction = 30 N

16. MCQs

Q1. Friction opposes:
A) Motion
B) Weight
C) Mass
D) Gravity
Answer: A) Motion
Q2. Limiting friction is proportional to:
A) Mass
B) Normal reaction
C) Velocity
D) Time
Answer: B) Normal reaction
Q3. Coefficient of friction is:
A) μ = N/F
B) μ = F/N
C) μ = F×N
D) μ = F+N
Answer: B) μ = F/N
Q4. Coefficient of friction has:
A) Unit of N
B) Unit of m
C) No unit
D) Unit of J
Answer: C) No unit
Q5. At limiting equilibrium:
A) Motion has stopped forever
B) Body is just about to move
C) No friction acts
D) Weight is zero
Answer: B) Body is just about to move
Q6. The relation between angle of repose and angle of friction is:
A) θ > φ
B) θ < φ
C) θ = φ
D) θ = 2φ
Answer: C) θ = φ

17. Quick Revision

  • Friction → Opposes relative motion.
  • Limiting friction → Maximum static friction.
  • F = μN
  • μ = F/N
  • μ has no unit.
  • tan φ = μ
  • tan θ = μ
  • Angle of repose = Angle of friction.
  • Horizontal plane: N = W
  • Inclined plane: N = W cos θ
