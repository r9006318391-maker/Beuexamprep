Unit 6 – Moment of Inertia

1. Introduction

The Moment of Inertia (M.I.) of an area about an axis is a measure of how the area is distributed with respect to that axis.

Moment of inertia is very important in the analysis of beams, shafts and structural members.

I = Σ(A × d²)

For a continuous area:

I = ∫ y² dA

2. Mass Moment of Inertia

Mass moment of inertia is the resistance of a body to angular acceleration about a particular axis.

I = ∫ r² dm

Where:

  • I = Mass moment of inertia
  • r = Distance from the axis
  • dm = Small element of mass

3. Area Moment of Inertia

Area moment of inertia is used mainly in engineering mechanics and structural analysis.

Ix = ∫ y² dA

Iy = ∫ x² dA

Here, x and y represent the perpendicular distances from the respective axes.

4. Unit and Dimension

For area moment of inertia:

SI Unit = m⁴

The dimensional formula is:

[L⁴]
Remember:
Area → m²
Moment of Area → m³
Moment of Inertia of Area → m⁴

5. M.I. of Rectangle

For a rectangle of breadth b and depth d:

About the Centroidal X-axis

Ix = bd³ / 12

About the Centroidal Y-axis

Iy = db³ / 12

6. M.I. of Circle

For a circular area of radius r:

Ix = Iy = πr⁴ / 4

In terms of diameter d:

I = πd⁴ / 64

7. M.I. of Triangle

For a triangle of base b and height h, the moment of inertia about its base is:

Ibase = bh³ / 12

About an axis through the centroid parallel to the base:

IG = bh³ / 36

8. Parallel Axis Theorem

The parallel axis theorem is used to find the moment of inertia about an axis parallel to the centroidal axis.

I = IG + Ah²

Where:

  • I = M.I. about the required axis
  • IG = M.I. about centroidal axis
  • A = Area of the body
  • h = Distance between the two parallel axes

9. Perpendicular Axis Theorem

For a plane lamina, the moment of inertia about an axis perpendicular to its plane is equal to the sum of the moments of inertia about two mutually perpendicular axes lying in the plane.

Iz = Ix + Iy
This theorem is applicable to plane areas.

10. Radius of Gyration

The radius of gyration is the distance from an axis at which the whole area or mass may be assumed to be concentrated without changing its moment of inertia.

I = Ak²

Therefore:

k = √(I/A)

Where:

  • k = Radius of gyration
  • I = Moment of inertia
  • A = Area

11. Important Formula Table

12. M.I. of Composite Areas

A complex area can be divided into simple shapes such as rectangles, triangles and circles.

Steps:

  1. Divide the given area into simple shapes.
  2. Find the area of each part.
  3. Locate the centroid of each part.
  4. Calculate the individual M.I.
  5. Apply the parallel axis theorem where required.
  6. Add the moments of inertia of all parts.
ITotal = Σ(IG + Ah²)

13. Solved Numerical

Question:
Find the moment of inertia of a rectangular area about its centroidal X-axis if its breadth is 100 mm and depth is 200 mm.

Given:

b = 100 mm
d = 200 mm

For a rectangle:

Ix = bd³ / 12

Substituting:

Ix = (100 × 200³) / 12
200³ = 8,000,000
Ix = 800,000,000 / 12
Ix = 66,666,666.67 mm⁴

Answer:

Moment of inertia = 6.667 × 10⁷ mm⁴
